Back in May I purchased my Salsa Journeyman, a gravel bike which I have since dubbed my "Salsa Verde" on [Strava](https://www.strava.com/athletes/86429743). This was an absolutely life-changing decision that has pushed me to go on longer and more adventurous ride than I ever would've imagined. But I simply would not have made this purchase this soon except for the fact that my beloved e-bike started to fail me. 

## The Good
Back in 2021, I bought a [Vanmoof S3](https://www.vanmoof.com/en-NL/our-rides/s3) smart e-bike. I chose that bike at the time, because I lived at my parents' house located at the top of a 200 foot climb, so the electric assist really helped to flatten that hill. I also was very concerned at the time about the safety and security of my bike, so I was drawn to the alarm and tracking features of the bike. All of these smart features were available for a quite affordable price for me at the time, especially from their outlet, where I bought the bike for $2,000. 

I really enjoyed that bike, using it for commuting around Burbank, for adventures across the county, and for getting to class at Cal Poly! Looking back to 2021 when I bought the bike, I was still so new in my riding journey! With each ride I went on with my Vanmoof, I pushed myself further. Starting initially with rides to thrift stores and coffee shops in Magnolia park, along with rides to North Hollywood on the Chandler Bike Path. At that time I had also been going to a local board games store, Geeky Teas and Games, located only a couple miles from my home.

![Vanmoof S3 at Griffith Observatory](/assets/images/03_Bike_Post/IMG_3964.jpeg)

For that stage in my bike riding journey, the electric assist really helped me to push my adventures further, and I was incredibly proud of this trip to the Griffith Observatory and Hollywood Sign. 

## The Bad
But all good things must come to the end, and the cracks in the company started to show about 1 year ago, when they filed for [bankruptcy](https://www.npr.org/2023/08/23/1195201692/vanmoof-ebikes-bankrupt-netherlands). The main issue that led to the company's end, and led to my abandonment of it, was its lack of repairability. 

Though as much as the assist helped me up the hills, the added weight of the motor and battery caused me to be especially timid on every decent through the park. While the motor helped me to keep speed with traffic on the road, I didn't always have confidence that I could stop in time, due in part because of the high speeds, but also the inconsistency of the brakes.

I personally experienced these issues when my e-shifter finally gave out about a couple of years ago. Their support was very kind, and sent me a replacement part. But in the long time that it took for that replacement to arrive, I had already brought it to the Vanmoof Repair Shop in Santa Monica. When my e-shifter went out again about a year ago, I luckily had a spare part to replace it with. While the process wasn't easy, with the correct replacement parts, it was possible for me to do myself. 

## The Straw That Broke the Camel's Back
By last May another issue started to appear in that my brakes started to feel loose. I tried replacing the brake pads, but that only helped marginally. I soon discovered that only the rear brake was functioning correctly and that the front brake was fully out. I had not done much regular maintenance on the bike and had no idea how to maintain the hydraulic brakes that the bike had. This lack of maintenance, along with the more intense hilly terrain in Southern California as compared to Vanmoof's home country of the Netherlands, it seemed to have put a lot of ware on the front brake caliper. 

These brake issues were further intensified by a crash on my commute to class down the hill from the Poly Canyon Village Apartments in my first year at Cal Poly. On my way to class, I waved to a friend as I sped down the hill by the parking garage. The road makes a tight, downhill curve at this location, and before I knew it, I was headed off the road at a high speed. Pulling the brakes did not help much, and I found myself jumping the curb and running straight into a streetlight pole.

This was not by first downhill crash on this bike, and in fact this theme of bad descents started the day I got the bike. After it arrived in the mail and I assembled it in the driveway, I took it out for a test ride around the neighborhood. I went up the hill and over a few blocks to coast down the hill and complete the square to my parent's house. It was in the approach to the stop sign at the street at the bottom of the hill that I realized that I had forgotten to put on a helmet. Not being used to the feeling of disc brakes, especially hydraulic disc brakes, I pulled the levers just a bit too hard and ended up flying forward over the handlebars on my new bike.

Trying to repair this issue on my own was a bit of a comedy of errors. I took my bike to the local bike kitchen, [Bike SLO County](https://bikeslocounty.org/programs/kitchen/), where the volunteer side eyed my fancy e-bike as I asked for help with what I thought would be a simple brake bleed. We worked together set up the funnel and syringe according to the pdf instructions I had saved from Vanmoof, and the mineral oil simply would not flow through the line. We attempted to trouble shoo this issue by replacing the brake line, and simply could not get the oil to flow. When this failed, we attempted to remove the brake line, but the [barb and olive](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z5qIrpGR3yY) were simply too strong. We kept tugging at the brake line connection to the brake lever on the handlebars but it simply wouldn't give. This resulted in a cascade of failures where the internally routed wires were severed, causing the dreaded error 40 and 41. After 3 hours of working a the issue, it was time to call it quits, I paid for my stand time at the bike kitchen, and walked my bike home. At that point, the magic of the so-called "smart bike" faded, and all of the technological innovation embedded in the bike became its greatest downfall. 

## Personal Growth and Evolving Needs
As mentioned above, the cracks in my relationship with my e-bike were starting to grow, and the repair issues were really just the straw that broke the camel's back. Since the time that I had purchased that bike, my life circumstances had changed and the e-bike life was no longer serving me.
1. For one, my commute in San Luis Obispo no longer includes as intense of a climb as my commute in Burbank. While I will relent that SLO does have some hills it has no where near the intensity of a climb as that slog up the Burbank Hills. While using the motor to go fast feels great, the best feature of e-bikes is their ability to flatten hills.
2. Secondly, the amazing safety features of the Vanmoof became a social taboo as I parked my bike next to other student's bikes on campus. I would sometimes come out to unlock my bike and would hear my bike's alarm go off.
3. Another issue I had was the immense weight of my ebike that weighed in at nearly 55lbs. This made the bike difficult to pick up and transport, especially if the elevator went out in the apartment building where I lived on the third floor. 4. Finally, I had goals to go on bigger and better rides that simply would not be possible with the limited range of an e-bike. Range anxiety is regularly discussed in context with electric cars, but also applies for bikes, where it can be even more devastating to be 40 miles from home and have to bike home with all the extra weight of the battery and motor.
